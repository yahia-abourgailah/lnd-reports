L&D Analytics Platform

What we are building, and what we need from you

Today the L&D report is built by hand in Excel. It takes one to two working days every cycle, it misses more than half of the training we actually deliver, and a few of the numbers in it are wrong. This is the plan to replace it.

Time needed 10 weeks + 1 preparation week Written 30 August 2026

The problem, in one page

Every reporting cycle, someone downloads attendance sheets from the CRM, pastes them into a spreadsheet, and types in seven pieces of information by hand for each training program. Then they refresh fourteen pivot tables and copy the results into a dashboard tab.

This takes eight to twelve hours. But the time is not the worst part. Three things are more serious:

25 of 55
Training programs that reach the report. The CRM holds 55. Leadership sees less than half of what L&D delivers.
3
Published numbers that are provably wrong. Not opinions — we can show the broken formulas in the file.
52
Attendance records that quietly disappear from sector reporting, because the person could not be matched to HR.

The three wrong numbers

Participation rate is too high. The formula divides by 192 people. That number is typed into the spreadsheet by hand and never changes. We actually have 212 employees. So the real participation rate is 60.4%, not the 66.7% we have been reporting.

Programs are being merged by accident. The report groups training by its title. But the CRM ran "Hard Talks" twice, as two separate programs. Because both have the same title, the spreadsheet treats them as one. Real deliveries are disappearing into each other.

Our NPS score is calculated the wrong way. NPS is a ratio, and ratios cannot be averaged. The spreadsheet averages them. This only gives the right answer if every group has exactly the same number of responses — which is never true. Every trainer and program comparison built on it is misweighted.

There is also a fourth, smaller one: one trainer's name is spelled two different ways ("Ahmed Elshiaty" and "Ahmed ElShiaty"), so his 41 evaluations are split into two people, and neither row shows his real performance.

Why this happens

None of this is anyone's mistake. It is what happens when a spreadsheet grows past what a spreadsheet can do. The file has no way to check itself, so when something goes wrong, it goes wrong quietly. The platform's main job is to stop things failing silently.

What we are building

A private website, used inside the company, that L&D logs into with their normal company account.

It connects directly to the CRM and to the HR system, and pulls the training data across automatically, every thirty minutes. Nobody downloads anything. Nobody pastes anything. The dashboard is simply always up to date.

On top of that data it shows the same numbers L&D reports today — programs, training days, participants, hours, participation rate, NPS, the four quality scores — plus the things Excel could never answer: which departments have had no training at all, how many people enrolled but did not show up, how each trainer is really performing, and how many people actually filled in the survey.

Every number can be clicked. Clicking it shows you the exact rows of data that produced it. If a figure looks wrong, you can see why in a few seconds instead of hunting through twelve worksheets.

Once a month, the report writes itself and emails itself to the recipient list. It arrives as an Excel file laid out like the one people already receive, so nobody outside L&D has to learn anything new.

The plan, stage by stage

Eleven weeks in total. One week of preparation, then ten weeks of building. Each stage only starts when the one before it is finished, so nothing is built on an assumption we have not checked.

Before we start
W0

Answering the open questions

Before any building, we need answers to a handful of questions about the CRM and the HR system. The biggest one: does the CRM actually allow another system to read its data automatically? If it does not, this becomes a longer project, and we need to know that now rather than in week eight.

Finished when: the questions are answered in writing and we can confirm the ten-week plan is real.

Weeks
1–2

Connecting to the CRM and HR

We build the connection that pulls training and employee data across automatically, and keeps pulling it every thirty minutes. We store an untouched copy of everything exactly as it arrives, so that if a number ever looks strange later, we can prove whether it arrived wrong or we broke it.

Finished when: both systems feed us on schedule, and if the CRM goes down the dashboard keeps showing the last good data instead of breaking.

Weeks
3–4

Fixing the numbers — and agreeing them with you

We organise the data properly and write each metric down once, in one place, so the dashboard, the Excel export and the tests can never disagree with each other. Every wrong calculation from the list above gets corrected here.

Then we sit down with L&D and go through every single number, old value against new value, with the reason it changed. This conversation happens in week 3, not at the end.

Finished when: L&D has seen every corrected number and accepted it in writing.

Weeks
5–6

The dashboard

The main screen with all the headline numbers, filters for date, sector, department, trainer and program, monthly trends, and the ability to click any number and see the records behind it. Plus a small admin screen where L&D fills in the two pieces of information the CRM does not hold.

Finished when: someone from L&D can reproduce their current dashboard on the new platform without help.

Weeks
7–8

The new analysis, and exports

The views Excel could never give us: a scorecard per program, a scorecard per trainer, a coverage view showing who has had no training, and the funnel from enrolled to attended to evaluated. Then the export buttons and the automatic monthly Excel report.

Finished when: every view is live, and the generated monthly report can be put side by side with the old spreadsheet.

Week
9

Catching problems, and switching on automation

We build the queue that catches bad records — a person we cannot match to HR, a session with no timing, someone scanned twice. Nothing is ever thrown away silently. And we switch on the scheduled email so the monthly report sends itself.

Finished when: the monthly report generates and sends with nobody touching it.

Week
10

Running both systems side by side, then handing over

For one full reporting cycle we run the platform and the spreadsheet together and compare them line by line. Every difference must have a reason. Then training, documentation, sign-off, and the old workbook is retired.

Finished when: L&D signs off, and the spreadsheet is closed for good.

The one difficult conversation

Please read this part

When we fix the broken calculations, some of our headline numbers will look worse. Participation rate drops from 66.7% to 60.4%. Some trainers' scores will move. Sector numbers will shift.

Nothing about our actual performance has changed. The training we delivered in February is exactly the same training. Only the arithmetic describing it has been corrected.

This is why we bring it to L&D in week 3, with the evidence in hand — the 192 typed into the spreadsheet, the two "Hard Talks" programs, the two spellings of one trainer's name. It is much easier to explain a correction in advance than to defend a surprise at launch.

There is also good news in the same direction. Because the platform reports all 55 programs instead of 25, several numbers will go up: total programs, total participants, total training hours. We have simply not been getting credit for a large part of the work.

What we need from you

Four decisions, before week 1

  • How should we count external attendees and new hires? 52 attendance records belong to people with no employee code. Should they count in the participation rate or not? (L&D Director)
  • Should the 30 missing programs be added to history? The CRM has 30 programs the spreadsheet never included. Do we go back and report them, or start fresh from launch? (L&D Director)
  • Which employee information may we store? We would like to leave out national ID and pay grade entirely, since no report needs them. (HR Leadership)
  • Scope or people. The ten weeks are completely full. We either drop the eight "nice to have" features, or add one more engineer. (L&D Director + Engineering Lead)

One day a week from an L&D specialist

This is the part most likely to be underestimated, so it is worth being direct about it. One named person from L&D needs one day per week for the whole project. They fill in the information the CRM does not hold, decide what to do with problem records, and test that the platform actually answers their real questions.

If that person is not available, weeks 5–6 and weeks 9–10 stall no matter how much progress engineering makes. Please name the person at kick-off and protect their time.

Access, in week 0

  • Read-only access to the CRM for the engineering team, and someone from the CRM side who can answer questions.
  • Read-only access to the HR system.
  • IT to set up the login so people sign in with their normal company account.

What could go wrong

Four honest risks, in the order we worry about them.

RiskWhat we do about it
The CRM cannot share its data automatically We find out in week 0, before the clock starts. If so, we fall back to reading the CRM's export file on a schedule. It still works, but data refreshes daily instead of every 30 minutes, and the project gets about three weeks longer.
There is no spare time in the ten weeks The work adds up to 44 person-weeks and the team supplies 45. That leaves no room for sickness, holidays or surprises. This is why we ask for the scope decision up front. It is the single most likely cause of a late delivery.
The corrected numbers are rejected We present them in week 3 with the evidence, not at the end. If they are not accepted by week 4 we stop and escalate, rather than building screens on numbers nobody will stand behind.
The CRM does not record who the trainer was We check this in week 0. If it does not, L&D will need to fill in the trainer for 55 programs by hand before launch, and we ask the CRM team to add the field later.

How we will know it worked

We are not finished when the software runs. We are finished when all of the following are true:

  • Every program in the CRM appears in the dashboard, and the count matches the CRM exactly.
  • L&D has signed off every corrected number.
  • Producing the monthly report takes under 30 minutes, and that time is spent checking, not assembling.
  • The monthly report has sent itself by email, with nobody involved, for one full cycle.
  • Any number on any screen can be clicked to show the records behind it.
  • No record is ever left out of a report without appearing in the problem queue.
  • Every screen loads in under two seconds.
  • The platform and the spreadsheet have run side by side for a full cycle, and every difference between them has a written explanation.

What is not included this time

These are deliberately left out of the first version. Not forgotten — the system is being designed so each can be added later without rebuilding anything.

Not includedWhy
Plan versus actualThere is no annual training plan stored anywhere in a structured form yet. The empty "Planned" column in the spreadsheet is the whole plan today.
LinkedIn LearningWe have no automatic data feed from it. Right now it is tracked on a hand-typed sheet.
Access for sector heads and executivesVersion 1 is for the L&D team only. Opening it wider means controlling who can see whose data, which is a piece of work in itself. Leadership receives exported reports instead.
Cost and return on investmentNeither the CRM nor HR holds any training cost data. There is nothing to calculate from.
Skills and certification trackingA different project. It needs a competency framework first.
Changing anything in the CRMDeliberate. The platform only ever reads. It can never edit or delete anything in the CRM, by design.

In short

Eleven weeks. Four engineers, plus one day a week from L&D. Four decisions needed before we start. At the end, the report builds itself, covers all our training instead of half of it, and every number in it can be checked by clicking on it.
