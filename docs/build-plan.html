01What we are building, and why

The report is assembled by hand in Excel: 8–12 hours per cycle, covering 25 of the 57 programs the CRM actually holds, with defects provable from the file. The replacement syncs from two sources — the CRM and a LinkedIn Learning export — computes every metric from one audited definition, and reports automatically. Read-only against the CRM — no write-back, enforced by the absence of a write client and by a read-only credential.

What the live connection proved — 2 September

The CRM exposes a single read-only endpoint, GET /api/learning-integration/programs, returning each program with its sessions, attendance, roster, survey answers and assessment answers nested inside. Authentication is a static service key in an X-Learning-Key header. We connected, pulled the whole dataset and landed it. All 57 programs validate against the typed models with zero rejections.

Measured, not estimated: 57 programs · 123 sessions · 1,048 roster entries · 53 programs carrying a survey · 16 distinct trainer strings · 5 distinct companies among attendees · 0 sessions missing a time · 0 roster rows without a matching CRM user.

The HRIS is not needed. The CRM user object already carried department, company and position; on request the CRM team added sector, job_level_name and job_level_grade. Both coverage dimensions in FR-D07 are now answerable from one source — 23 sectors and 17 job grades, from CEO down to Clerk. It also exposes no national ID and no payscale field, so the PII allowlist has nothing to reject.

Identity is settled. Across 2,218 user objects the payload carries 417 people, 417 employee codes and 417 odoo ids — a perfect 1:1, none missing, no code carrying two names. FR-B04's four-step resolution collapses to a single join, and P-07 closes: the workbook's 38 unjoinable rows were an artefact of its export, not a property of the source. The one exception is the trainer, who is the only person in the payload represented by a typed name rather than a key.

P-05 has reappeared, in sector. 940 of 1,052 user objects carry a trailing space: 28 distinct raw values collapse to 23 once trimmed, so grouping on the raw value invents five sectors that do not exist. Exactly the defect that split Projects from Projects  in the workbook. Two smaller ones alongside it: job_level_grade arrives as text, so "10" sorts before "9" and seniority ordering breaks unless it is parsed to an integer; and job level is absent for 74 people, roughly one in ten, who therefore drop out of level breakdowns.

The trap to know about. status and computed_status disagree on 55 of 57 programs — every one of them stored as upcoming while actually completed. Counting Total Programs off status yields 2; the real answer is 55. Only computed_status is trustworthy.

P-12 is closed. Sessions carry a stable id, so Training Days is a real COUNT(DISTINCT session_key) and the workbook's # column is retired rather than reconciled.

Deploy and rollback

  1. CI builds both images, tags with the commit SHA, pushes to the registry.
  2. Migrations run as a one-shot container first — compose run --rm api alembic upgrade head. If it fails, the deploy stops and the running version is untouched.
  3. compose up -d recreates changed services; db and redis are not recreated. Health polled, then previous images pruned.
  4. Images are built once and promoted — what was tested on staging is bit-identical to production. Rollback re-deploys the previous SHA; migrations stay backward-compatible for one release so no database downgrade is ever needed.

Network, secrets, backup

  • Only 443 published. Egress allowlisted to the CRM, the LinkedIn export host, the IdP and SMTP. Corporate network or VPN only.
  • Every credential from the company secret store as an environment variable at deploy. None in source, images or Compose files. Secret scanning on every push.
  • Nightly pg_dump -Fc plus WAL archiving, 30-day retention. Restore rehearsed for real in week 10 — configured is not proven.

Two hour metrics, one name

Program Duration/hrs = 130.5 is catalogue hours: how much distinct training was built. Learning Hrs = 1,386 is learner-hours: hours times the people who received them. Both correct, for different questions. Named Training Hours Delivered and Learner Hours so they are never conflated again.

Sync

  • Incremental every 30 min on updated_at, watermark per entity, 5-minute overlap for clock skew.
  • Full reconcile nightly — soft-deletes what vanished, reprocesses changed hashes. Catches what incremental cannot see.
  • Idempotent by construction. Transform is a pure function of raw plus enrichment, upserted on natural key. Re-running any window is a no-op.
  • Last known good. A failed pull never triggers a transform; the dashboard keeps serving and the freshness banner shows the lag.

05The metric layer

Nine KPIs currently live inside nine unauditable GETPIVOTDATA strings. Six of them are wrong — participation rate twice over (P-01, P-13), and NPS plus three quality scores through one hidden filter (P-03). One definition, declared once, consumed identically by API, exports and tests.

The type system prevents P-03 recurring

Correction to the premise. NPS was not wrong because a ratio was averaged. In the workbook every row scores exactly +1, 0 or −1, so averaging them already equals (promoters − detractors) / n. The published figure was wrong because it was computed over a silently filtered 55 of 77 responses.

RatioMetric is still worth building — it keeps group-level aggregation correct once responses per group differ, which they will. But the defence that actually prevents P-03 recurring is explicit scope: every metric declares its population, any filter is a named parameter rather than an inherited one, and the response count is returned beside every quality score so a shrinking denominator is visible. Each metric also carries its definition string, served to the UI and stamped on every export — the number, its definition and its population ship together.

08Quality, security, operations

The invariant that makes silent loss impossible

Every transform run asserts count(raw active) = count(core included) + count(quarantined) before it commits. Off by one and the run fails loudly, with the dashboard still serving the previous good state. That turns a policy into a property.

Eleven detection rules

IDENTITY_UNRESOLVED (0 today — the code join is 1:1) · TRAINER_MISSING · CUSTOMISED_DEPT_MISSING · DURATION_UNDERIVABLE · ATTENDANCE_NO_ENROLLMENT · EVALUATION_NO_ATTENDANCE · DUPLICATE_ATTENDANCE · CAPACITY_EXCEEDED · PROGRAM_TYPE_MISMATCH (advisory) · LINKEDIN_LEARNER_UNMATCHED · ATTENDEE_OUTSIDE_ROSTER. Eleven, not thirteen: EVAL_PROGRAM_UNMATCHED and EVAL_TRAINER_UNMATCHED existed only to catch free-text Forms responses failing to match a CRM program or trainer. Answers now arrive already attached to their program and respondent, so neither failure mode exists.

Measured against live CRM data, before any transform: TRAINER_MISSING would raise 2 (sessions with no trainer name); DURATION_UNDERIVABLE would raise 0; IDENTITY_UNRESOLVED would raise 0 — every roster row resolves to a CRM user. The workbook's 38 unjoinable rows were a workbook artefact, not a source defect. Each record is still counted or excepted, never neither.

Security

  • Field minimisation. Moot at source: the CRM exposes neither identification_id nor any payscale field, and there is no HRIS integration to leak them. The rejected-set test remains as a regression guard, and adding any field still requires naming the metric that needs it.
  • SSO only, no local passwords. One L&D role in v1, claims carried so row-level scoping is additive.
  • Read-only at source — no CRM write client exists in the codebase, and credentials are issued read-only.
  • Containers non-root, slim base, no build toolchain in the runtime layer, image scanning in CI.
  • Audit — enrichment changes and exception resolutions attributed and retained.

Alerts and the monthly routine

Alert on: any sync failure or two missed runs · freshness lag over 60 min · any transform invariant failure (a correctness alarm, not a capacity one) · over 25 open exceptions or any older than 14 days · p95 over 2s sustained · report not delivered within 30 min of schedule.

After handover the cycle is: clear the queue (10 min), complete missing enrichment (10 min), check completeness and freshness (5 min), and the report sends itself. 25 minutes against 8–12 hours today.

How to present it

Frame it as correcting a known defect, never as a decline. The training delivered in February did not change; only the arithmetic describing it did. Bring the evidence — the hardcoded 192, the two Hard Talks IDs, the two spellings of one trainer's name, and the pivot filter that drops the two largest programs from every quality score — and let the file make the argument.

The quality scores need care: they fall because 29% of the feedback was excluded, not because satisfaction dropped. Say that first, then show the numbers.

Cutover: week 9 the platform generates alongside the workbook but distributes nothing · week 10 full parallel cycle, every difference attributed · Director signs the reconciliation · distribution switches to the platform · the workbook is frozen read-only and the SOP retired · it stays restorable for one further cycle, and reverting means resuming manual assembly, so the trigger is a correctness failure, not a preference.

Accepted when

  1. Every CRM program appears, count matching the CRM exactly — automated nightly check on the health page.
  2. Every KPI computes to a value L&D signed in the reconciliation, and participation rate is published only once its numerator and denominator cover the same entity set.
  3. The monthly report takes under 30 minutes of human work — timed observation in week 10.
  4. It has generated and delivered by email with no human step for one full cycle.
  5. Any KPI drills to its source records — E2E test asserts the drill count equals the KPI at every grain.
  6. No record excluded without an exception — the transform invariant, alarmed on failure.
  7. Every view returns within 2 seconds at p95 against 15,000 rows.
  8. The metric suite passes on the release commit.
  9. Coverage, funnel and scorecards live and exportable.
  10. A full parallel run completed with all differences explained.
